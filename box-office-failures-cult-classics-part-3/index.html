Box Office Failures That Became Cult Classics, Part 3

Andaz Apna Apna, Jaane Bhi Do Yaaro, Bhavesh Joshi Superhero and more: films we ignored on release and could not stop rewatching.

By Sai Charan, 28 Dec 2023, 6 min read

A truly Indian superhero film nobody gave a damn about. A legendary comedy with two superstars that took more than three years to make. A modern-day classic I was not allowed to watch as a kid because of all the kissing. This is part three of box office failures that became cult classics.

1. Andaz Apna Apna (1994)

Putting two stars in the same film today feels like an impossible dream for most studios, except a few like Yash Raj, who have the firepower and the budgets. But thirty years ago, Aamir and Salman dropped most of their egos and joined forces for the most nonsensical, irreverent slapstick comedy of all time, and I mean that in the best way.

A younger Hindi audience may know it only through its countless memes, or through Crime Master Gogo. The tone and the deliberate overacting may not land for them. But for the generation of my elder brothers and sisters, this film was a bible, and they can recite every reference, joke and line like religious devotees. Clever dialogue, unforgettable characters, actors who refuse to take themselves seriously for once, and clean humour with no sexism or double meaning.

Maybe it failed because it was stuck in production for three years over scheduling conflicts and star egos. Maybe audiences were not ready for a full-blown comedy with self-deprecating characters. Today it is the prime example of an infinitely rewatchable cult classic, in an industry that has almost forgotten how to make comedies.

2. Jaane Bhi Do Yaaro (1983)

By his own confession, even Naseeruddin Shah did not get the humour Kundan Shah was going for while shooting, and audiences felt the same on release. After watching the final cut, Naseer realised what Kundan was making, and that it would be way ahead of its time. That is still true.

Disguised as a comedy, it is one of the greatest satires on corruption in every part of India’s system and society. It is doubtful it could be made today. It is also supremely rewatchable, with one of the most hilarious climaxes in Indian cinema, set in a stage play of the Mahabharata, which will make you wonder how Bollywood comedy went from that to this.

3. Bhavesh Joshi Superhero (2018)

There is a young audience for big-scale superhero films like the Krrish franchise, but the superhero genre in India is full of poor copies of Hollywood. In my humble opinion, only two films manage original, organic, rooted, truly Indian superhero stories: Minnal Murali, the hugely successful Malayalam film on Netflix, and the not-so-successful Bhavesh Joshi Superhero, a flawed masterpiece by Vikramaditya Motwane, probably our most versatile filmmaker today.

Yes, it is not perfect. The lead and the second half have issues, and the climax is a downer. But the rest is so relatable and relevant, especially five years after release: the genesis of a superhero from two friends starting a vigilante YouTube channel. In school, after we tried to deal with the terrible condition of the road next to us, my friends and I wanted to start our own vigilante club exposing the local municipality and the system itself. We never did, which is why it was so validating to see that failed dream come alive in a film built around the Mumbai water crisis.

I love the intent to blend superhero genre and politics, a hero who stops at a red signal even during a midnight chase, the stylish yet grounded action, the gritty tone, and frames bursting with innocence and honesty. It is a modern cult film I will always be proud of, and always be heartbroken that we failed to support.

4. Kabhi Haan Kabhi Naa (1994)

In 2023 alone, after returning from a hiatus, Shah Rukh Khan is at a new peak of stardom, working with the biggest directors and playing raw agents, superheroes and vigilante killers. But thirty years ago, before he was a superstar, he played a young loser in a film without much of a plot. Sunil is a boy refusing to become an adult, constantly failing exams, obsessed with his music and his crush Anna. He is easily the most charming and relatable character Shah Rukh has ever played, in the most misunderstood rom-com in Bollywood, where, spoiler alert, the boy does not even get the girl.

That is probably why the audience of the time did not give it wholehearted love. You can watch it any number of times for its calm, chilled-out vibe: a pre-Dil Chahta Hai Goa, a low-stakes story without a single negative character, legendary music, and a flawed, unserious Shah Rukh playing completely against the hero image the world knows today. From the opening shot of him riding a motorcycle singing his heart out, to the end credits, it is a classic feel-good Hindi film.

5. Manorama Six Feet Under (2007)

Nepotism plagues every professional field, including film, where access and opportunity go to those born into film families. Star kids cannot choose their families, but every decade a handful accept their privilege and work on their craft. A shining example is an actor who tried to shed his legacy and created a sub-genre for himself, taking on projects nobody else was doing: the neo-noir murder mystery Manorama Six Feet Under.

It is an underrated gem, streaming free on YouTube. Grab some snacks and enjoy the slow-burn, atmospheric ride. Whenever I think of Abhay Deol’s choices, I wonder whether he could have made them without his family’s legacy, and I also see an actor who, despite that legacy and his unique choices, was sidelined by the very industry he was trying to lift.

6 and 7. Maqbool (2003) and Omkara (2006)

The next two films have a lot in common: both directed by the master storyteller Vishal Bhardwaj. Most of our popular filmmakers pick up a DVD and copy-paste it into an unofficial remake. Bhardwaj takes the skeleton of Shakespeare, the core emotions of greed, lust and jealousy, and adds flesh and blood with multi-dimensional grey characters, intricate screenplays, spectacular dialogue and timeless music, in a way that would make Shakespeare wake up and say well done.

Both have fantastic performances from every cast member. Omkara especially shocked us by presenting Saif Ali Khan in a way we had never seen, hitting it out of the stadium as the cunning, scheming Langda Tyagi. Neither worked at the box office at first. Both slowly built a cult following over the years.

8. Hey Ram (2000)

I was nine when Hey Ram came out, and as kids we were not allowed anywhere near it because of the kissing and the violence. Maybe most of the audience thought that was what the film was about, which may be why they refused to watch. Those who did may have found it too long, too complicated, too dark or too intellectual. I cannot say it would be appreciated if it released today. Honestly, it would be boycotted left, right and centre.

Kamal Haasan’s films being ahead of their time or misunderstood is a whole other list, but near the top is this huge, ambitious masterpiece, written and directed by the man himself. It talks about friendship, freedom, religion, identity and post-traumatic stress, and every rewatch reveals another layer. It had Shah Rukh Khan sharing screen space with Kamal Haasan, a haunting album by Ilaiyaraaja, three National Awards, and it was touted as one of the most expensive films of its time. If there were protests against its release over twenty years ago, there is no way it would be accepted today.

And that was part three of box office failures that became cult classics.
