10 Indian Filmmakers Who Redefined Cinema and Have Taken a Backseat

Ten directors across Tamil, Telugu and Hindi cinema whose best work is behind them, or whose next film is still awaited.

By Sai Charan, 12 Dec 2024, 3 min read

The director is often called the captain of the ship. Every director carries a signature, a distinct style, and sensibilities that evolve with each project. As movie lovers, we gravitate toward the ones who resonate with our tastes.

Here are ten talented Indian filmmakers who made exceptional cinema in the past but have been relatively inactive in recent years. They are personal favourites of mine. Some are on a break, and some are working on their next big thing.

1. Mysskin (Tamil)

Mysskin makes films that go past storytelling and reach the audience at a subconscious level. Strong emotion, dark humour and philosophical undertones, delivered through visual storytelling. From Pisasu to Psycho, his films explore different genres and all carry his signature. His framing, rhythm and editing set him apart.

Psycho came out four years ago. He is now working on Pisasu 2 and Train, a film with Vijay Sethupathi.

2. Bala (Tamil)

Bala makes raw, intense films that go deep into the darker side of human emotion. From Sethu through Pithamagan and Naan Kadavul, his work is deeply impactful and often disturbing in its realism. He has been criticised for repetitive tragic endings, but he never shies away from challenging narratives. The teaser for Vanangaan hints at a return to his signature style.

3. Thiagarajan Kumararaja (Tamil)

Known for non-linear storytelling and a unique visual language, Kumararaja has directed only two features, Aaranya Kaandam and Super Deluxe, and both are masterpieces. His films explore relationships, morality and existential themes inside gripping narratives. He recently directed an episode of Modern Love: Chennai, and fans are still waiting for the next feature.

4. Balaji Sakthivel (Tamil)

Balaji Sakthivel is celebrated for hard-hitting, realistic cinema. Kaadhal and Vazhakku Enn 18/9 take on societal issues in an almost documentary style. He has recently moved into acting, and many hope he returns to direction. His films remain unforgettable.

5. Chandrasekhar Yeleti (Telugu)

Aithe remains one of Telugu cinema’s most philosophical and well-written films. Yeleti focuses on human values and relationships, with Anukokunda Oka Roju and Manamantha among his works. His last film, Atagadara Siva, released in 2018. As Telugu cinema tilts towards fast-paced entertainers, many hope he returns with another thoughtful story.

6. A. Karunakaran (Telugu)

Karunakaran is best known for feel-good love stories, starting with the iconic Tholi Prema and continuing with Ullasanga Utsahanga and Darling. His films have clean humour, meaningful narratives and well-written female characters. It has been six years since his last film, and he could stage a strong comeback with a fresh approach.

7. V.V. Vinayak (Telugu)

Vinayak is synonymous with commercial mass entertainers in Telugu cinema. From Aadi to Adhurs, his films are staples of Telugu masala cinema. His last Telugu film came out over six years ago, and his Hindi remake of Chatrapathi did not fare well. Fans still hope for a grand Telugu comeback from the once-reigning director of mass films.

8. Farhan Akhtar (Hindi)

Farhan redefined Bollywood with Dil Chahta Hai, which introduced a new-age storytelling style, and showed versatility with Lakshya and Don. He has since shifted focus to acting. His last film as director, Don 2, was released in 2011. There are rumours about Don 3, but many of us are curious about his next original project.

9. Ashutosh Gowariker (Hindi)

Gowariker is known for historical dramas like Lagaan and Jodhaa Akbar, and for blending scale with emotion. His films are rooted in Indian history and culture, with relatable, multi-dimensional characters. Panipat was a setback, but audiences remain optimistic about a comeback.

10. Anand Gandhi (Hindi)

Anand Gandhi made waves with Ship of Theseus, a thought-provoking film that redefined independent Indian cinema. His contribution to Tumbbad cemented his reputation as a visionary. He is currently working on a horror film, Wild Beasts.

Who comes back first?

These filmmakers left an indelible mark on Indian cinema with unique voices. Some have taken a backseat, and others are gearing up for what comes next. Which one is your favourite, and whose comeback are you most excited about?
